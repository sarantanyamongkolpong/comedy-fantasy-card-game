# Agent 02 — Western fantasy monster research

Status: RESEARCH. This folder is notes for a later design pass. Nothing here is approved, locked, or a production asset. Pitches in `pitches.json` are AGENT_CREATED and marked PROPOSED. Facts live only in `inspirations.json`.

Accessed date on every source is 2026-11-20. Each URL was fetched in this pass (Wikipedia lead extracts, Tolkien Gateway and UESP and Warcraft wiki.gg MediaWiki leads, Diablo and Witcher fandom section-0 wikitext, the Wizards SRD page, and the two SRD 5.1 PDFs). A fact is not claimed unless that page's lead, or a sentence that names the creature in the PDF, says it.

`publicDomain: true` is used only for traditional folklore and classical myth, and for literary creatures whose authors have been dead for centuries (Beowulf, Ariosto's hippogriff). It is not used for CC-BY text. A Creative Commons license is not the public domain.

`protectedName: true` means the name was coined for a modern work, or the work treats it as its own signature creature, and a card should not use that name. Ordinary English words that a game also uses (cliff racer, lurcher, scamp, noonwraith) are flagged false, with a note that the game depiction is still not free to copy.

## Archetypes worth stealing as ideas

These are shapes, not names.

- **The weapon is the exit.** Bonnacon, and the later joke of a knight who only fights backward.
- **A creature that is also a bad theory.** Barnacle goose (born from shells), bestiary panther (sweet roar, three-day nap), amphisbaena (a head at each end).
- **The healer who leaves.** Caladrius takes the sickness and flies toward or away from the patient.
- **The floor is the monster.** Aspidochelone is an island that is a fish. The mimic is a later game version of "the object is the monster."
- **Gaze and breath.** Basilisk, cockatrice, catoblepas: death is a look or a downward breath. Hydra's blood is poison that lingers.
- **One piece too many or too few.** Fachan (one of each limb), cyclops (three different one-eyed traditions), chimera and manticore (stacked animals), hydra (heads that answer an injury).
- **Water that is a horse, a dog, or a person.** Kelpie, each-uisge, nuckelavee, boobrie, selkie, mermaid. Kelpie and each-uisge are close and must stay two records: the each-uisge page calls it the fiercer cousin.
- **The headless errand.** Dullahan. Confidence is medium because the creature's own article doubts how well Irish oral tradition attests it.
- **Light that works for a wage.** Bluecap, will-o'-the-wisp, knocker. The Warcraft candle-kobold is a different joke (see doubles below).
- **The mound does not stay dead.** Saga draugr, then Tolkien's barrow-wight, which the sources say he drew from that idea.
- **The house spirit with a contract.** Brownie, German kobold, boggart, hobgoblin, nisse. Help until the terms change.
- **The world is an animal.** Jörmungandr bites its tail around the world. The stoor worm's teeth become islands. Fenrir is the wolf at the end of the gods. These are public domain. Do not dress them as a licensed boss.
- **The thing under the floor.** Warcraft Old Gods are a licensed version of "the buried horror is the landlord." Use the public-domain world-serpent or a bestiary fish-island instead of those names.
- **The disguise is furniture or luggage.** Mimic. The pitch in this folder uses a luggage cart on purpose, so it is not a treasure chest.
- **The ooze is a room.** Gelatinous cube. A parody should not be a perfect ten-foot cube.
- **The animal that eats your equipment.** Rust monster. The joke is the inventory, not the body. The body was not fully described in the lead used here.
- **The swarm that follows you home.** Cliff racer. A legless flying reptile that waits until there are enough of them.
- **The machine whose owners left.** Dwemer animunculi still guard ruins and repair themselves.
- **The ghost that recruits.** Noonwraith: grain moves with no wind, and the dance is the fight.

## Product identity versus public domain

### D&D, from the books themselves

Two PDFs were downloaded and read:

- OGL SRD 5.1: `https://media.wizards.com/2016/downloads/DND/SRD-OGL_V5.1.pdf`
- CC-BY SRD 5.1: `https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf`
- Wizards' own SRD page: `https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document`

The OGL PDF's preamble says the following are not Open Game Content: beholder, gauth, carrion crawler, tanar'ri, baatezu, displacer beast, githyanki, githzerai, mind flayer, illithid, umber hulk, yuan-ti. It says the rest of that SRD is Open Game Content under the OGL.

The same page says SRD 5.1 was also released under CC-BY 4.0, and that SRD 5.2 is the current document. The CC-BY release is a license with an attribution requirement. It does not make those monsters public domain, and it does not put the Product Identity names into the SRD as monsters you can reprint.

The Wizards page says the beholder was left out of SRD 5.2 because it is part of D&D's brand identity. The CC-BY 5.1 PDF still contains the word "beholder" in the Deck of Illusions table ("Ace of diamonds Beholder"). That is a name in a list of illusions, not a license to use the monster.

| Name | In the OGL "not Open Content" list | Public domain | What this pass actually verified |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beholder | yes | no | Floating orb, central eye, eyestalks, many magical abilities. Do not draw this. |
| Mind flayer / illithid | yes | no | Psionic humanoid aberration that eats brains and keeps thralls. The lead used here does not describe the face. Do not invent one. |
| Displacer beast | yes | no | Only "evil feline," created for the game. Confidence medium. No tentacles were in the fetched text. |
| Githyanki / githzerai | yes | no | Humanoids, ancient enemies of each other. No armor or mounts were verified. |
| Yuan-ti | yes | no | Evil snakelike people in castes, with a human-cult origin in some settings. |
| Carrion crawler | yes | no | Name only. Appearance was not on the page the title redirected to. |
| Umber hulk | yes | no | Name only. Same problem. |
| Gauth, tanar'ri, baatezu | yes | no | Named in the OGL preamble. No creature record, because no description was fetched. |

These are in the SRD text and were not on that Product Identity list. They are still game monsters, `publicDomain: false`.

| Name | What was verified | Parody caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mimic | Shapechanges into an object, often a chest; adhesive; pseudopods | Do not draw a treasure chest with a mouth. |
| Gelatinous cube | A cube of transparent ooze that engulfs and digests; the SRD uses one as a dungeon surprise | Do not draw a ten-foot cube. |
| Owlbear | Bear mixed with owl; hugs; beak. Gygax traced it to a plastic toy | The hybrid is the game's signature. |
| Rust monster | Eats metal, especially weapons and armor; usually not a killer | The joke is gear, not a copied body. |
| Lich | Undead spellcaster who used magic to stay. The word is an old English word for a corpse | The word is old. The phylactery-wizard package was not in the lead used here, so it is not claimed. |
| Otyugh | Sends simple messages and images within 120 feet, and the other creature cannot answer the same way. The action line also names a bite and tentacles | The PDF columns are interleaved, so no size or damage numbers are taken from that block. |
| Cloaker | Makes three illusory copies when it is not in bright light; they vanish in bright light. Motionless, with the underside hidden, it looks like a dark leather cloak | No damage numbers. The look stops at that sentence. |

PDF columns in the SRD are interleaved. Stat lines were not trusted. Only sentences that name the creature were used.

### Warcraft, Diablo, Elder Scrolls, Witcher, Tolkien

All of these records are `publicDomain: false`.

Tolkien died on 2 September 1973 (the Wikipedia biography lead). That is why these are not folklore. This note does not guess a copyright expiry year.

Do not use, or closely rename: murloc, quilboar, worgen, core hound, n'raqi, furbolg, nerubian, Old God and the four names, voidwalker, Treasure Goblin, khazra, Cow King, Hell Bovine, Moophisto, sand maggot as a Diablo proper name, lacuni, dremora, kyn, clannfear, hagraven, chaurus, Dwemer, bruxa, shaelmaar, balrog, Nazgûl, Ringwraith, Ent as Tolkien's tree-shepherd, Treebeard, warg as an orc-mount, barrow-wight, oliphaunt, mûmak, Uruk-hai, Watcher in the Water as the Moria pool scene.

The English word "ent" meant giant. The talking tree-people are Tolkien's. The English word "warg" sits on older Norse and Old English words; the wolf that orcs ride is Tolkien's. "Oliphaunt" is a Middle English word he used for Sam's voice; the mûmak is his beast. "Orc" in the modern fantasy sense is a later literary creature; the Wikipedia title "Uruk-hai" resolved to a generic orc article and was not used. The Uruk-hai record cites Tolkien Gateway only.

Warcraft's "you no take candle" line and the gurgling cry are not copied into any pitch.

## Doubles that are not the same creature

- **Kobold.** INS-02-017 is the German household spirit. INS-02-079 is Warcraft's ratlike mine humanoid with a candle on its head.
- **Leshy / leshen.** INS-02-098 is the Slavic forest tutelary, public domain. INS-02-099 is the Witcher wiki's "lives only to kill" monster. The game lead did not describe antlers or a skull. Do not add them.
- **Spriggan.** The Witcher page uses "spriggan" as another name for its leshen. A Cornish spriggan lead was too thin to record. They are not merged.
- **Draugr and barrow-wight.** INS-02-018 is the saga revenant. INS-02-109 is Tolkien's wight, which the sources say comes from that idea. The UESP draugr page returned an empty extract and was not used.
- **Warg and Fenrir.** INS-02-050 is the Norse wolf. INS-02-108 is Tolkien's mount.
- **Vampire.** INS-02-040 is the European folklore corpse: shrouded, bloated, ruddy or dark. The pale gentleman is a 19th-century look. Bruxa (INS-02-104) is a game vampire and a different record.
- **Succubus.** A folklore page was fetched and not given a record, because the useful card ideas were already covered and the pitch set should not be sexual. A Diablo succubus lead was fetched and also left out for the same reason.
- **Gargoyle.** INS-02-060 is a stone waterspout. It does not say the statue wakes up.
- **Griffin and hippogriff.** The griffin is ancient. The hippogriff is Ariosto, early 16th century, and public domain as a literary creature, not as a game mount.

## Confidence

High unless noted.

- INS-02-012 Dullahan: the article itself questions the Irish attestation.
- INS-02-063 Displacer beast: the fetched text supports "evil feline" and the product-identity status, not a body plan.
- INS-02-071 Carrion crawler and INS-02-072 Umber hulk: product-identity names only.
- INS-02-073 Otyugh: the telepathy sentence, including the rule that the other creature cannot reply in kind, is complete. Bite and tentacles are named in the action line. The rest of that stat block is mixed with neighboring monsters, so it is not used.
- INS-02-099 Leshen: the game lead is one harsh sentence. The folklore record is the one to design from.
- INS-02-113 Fell beast: Tolkien Gateway's lead is one sentence calling them pterodactyl-like steeds of the Nazgûl.

These were fetched and then left out because the lead was a disambiguation, a redirect to the wrong article, or too thin to support a creature: Diablo's Butcher and Fallen and Grotesque, UESP atronach (disambiguation), UESP mudcrab and nix-hound and draugr (empty extracts), Witcher nekker and chort, Yale, Lambton Worm, Questing Beast, Wolpertinger, bulette (disambiguation). Absence from the file means "not verified," not "does not exist."

## Pitches

`pitches.json` has 12 original proposals, ids PITCH-02-001 through PITCH-02-012. Categories: three Normal, three Elite, two Mini Boss, one Main Boss, one World Boss, two Secret. They use public-domain shapes except the luggage-cart elite, which is flagged medium because the adhesive object is a mimic idea, and which is deliberately not a chest.

No pitch uses a protected name. No pitch gives numbers for health or damage. Suggested rarities are only N, R, Epic, or EX, matching the game's avatar rarities. Factions mentioned are the four existing ones or the tower roles already in the lore (Wild Creature, Corrupted Echo, Tower Guardian, tower anomaly).

## What not to draw, even as a joke

- A floating orb with a central eye and eyestalks.
- An octopus-headed humanoid. (Not even verified here. Still the mind flayer's signature in the culture, so stay away.)
- A fire-shadow demon with a many-thonged whip.
- Nine cloaked riders.
- A treasure chest with a tongue.
- A perfect transparent cube of slime.
- A fish-man crowd, a candle-headed rat miner, a two-headed lava mastiff.
- A goat-man whose backstory is a wizard order's failed weapon.
- A cow king and a pun on a demon lord's name.
- A pool of tentacles outside a mine gate. The public-domain sea version is the kraken, which is a different place and a different story.
