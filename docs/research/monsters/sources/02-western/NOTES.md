# Agent 02 — Western fantasy monster research

Status: RESEARCH. Not QA_PASSED, not LOCKED, not OWNER_APPROVED.

Scope owned by this branch: inspirations a card-game parody can point at without copying a protected name or a signature silhouette. Covered traditions: medieval bestiaries and European folklore already common in Western fantasy; D&D with the SRD/OGL versus Wizards product-identity split; Warcraft, Diablo, Tolkien, Elder Scrolls, and The Witcher as game bestiaries.

World fit, read only: comedy fantasy, Mysterious Tower as the PvE place, Echo hybrids of real people, factions Aegis Dominion (tank), Phantom Blades (dps), Celestial Nexus (magic), Sanctuary Order (support). Monsters may stay wild, corrupted Echo, tower guardian, or tower anomaly. This folder does not change that lore.

## How a record was allowed in

Every `sources[].url` was fetched on 2026-11-20. A fact is included only when that page states it. `confidence` is `medium` when the page itself is thin or disputes the tradition (see the dullahan). `publicDomain: true` here means the creature is a traditional folklore or classical-myth being, not that a later game's art of it is free to copy. `protectedName: true` is reserved for coined product-identity or franchise names, recorded in later entries.

## Batch 1 archetypes (folklore)

- Bestiary anatomy jokes: bonnacon, cockatrice, manticore, catoblepas, tarasque, fachan, boobrie, Lou Carcolh.
- False ground and false light: aspidochelone, will-o'-the-wisp, bluecap.
- Water horses, ranked by how badly the ride ends: kelpie, each-uisge, nuckelavee, knucker, afanc.
- Household labor spirits: kobold, brownie. They are not mine mascots.
- Barrow and omen dead: draugr, banshee, sluagh, Black Shuck, dullahan.
- Skin and shape: selkie, púca.
- A healer bird and a bird that is not born from an egg: caladrius, barnacle goose.
- A world-sized corpse: stoor worm (Orkney telling of the world-serpent idea).

## IP cautions already visible

Folklore names in this batch (banshee, kelpie, kobold, draugr, and the rest) are not a company's product identity. Later games reused several of them. The reusable part is the old story. The not-reusable part is a specific game's costume, catchphrase, or stat-block wording.

The dullahan's own article says the headless coachman is poorly attested in Irish oral sources. Treat that entry as a famous image with a shaky paper trail, not as a well-documented medieval type.

Kelpie and each-uisge are easy to merge by accident. The kelpie article glosses each-uisge as a Gaelic name for the kelpie. The each-uisge article calls it a similar, more vicious water horse. They stay two records.

Game bestiaries, Tolkien creatures, and the D&D product-identity list are the next batch. The OGL preamble of System Reference Document 5.1 names these as not Open Content: beholder, gauth, carrion crawler, tanar'ri, baatezu, displacer beast, githyanki, githzerai, mind flayer, illithid, umber hulk, yuan-ti. Source fetched 2026-11-20: https://media.wizards.com/2016/downloads/DND/SRD-OGL_V5.1.pdf. The same SRD 5.1 text was also released under CC-BY-4.0 (https://media.wizards.com/2023/downloads/dnd/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf and https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document). CC-BY is a license, not the public domain, and it does not put those product-identity monster names into this game. Pitches stay out until those records exist, so a joke is not stuck to a name we cannot verify.
