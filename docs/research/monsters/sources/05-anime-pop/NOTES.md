# Agent 05 notes — anime, pop culture, urban legends, historical legends

Status: **RESEARCH**. Not QA_PASSED. Not owner-approved. Nothing here changes Mysterious Tower lore, faction names, or combat numbers.

This lane covers villain, creature, and boss **archetypes**. Specific franchises are named only so a later pass can see what is protected. Parody names belong in `pitches.json` and, later, with agents 06–08. They are not the original names.

## How the records work

`inspirations.json` is a JSON array in the brief's format, plus `sensitivity`.

- `facts` are only claims checked against a page fetched on 2026-11-20. The fetch was the Wikipedia plain-text extract for that URL. If a famous detail was not in that extract, it is not in the fact list. Attack on Titan's anatomy is the clearest example: the article supports walled cities, man-eating humanoids, Pure versus Abnormal Titans, and grappling gear. It did not state a weak point, so none is recorded.
- `protectedName: true` means do not use that name, or the character's signature look, as if it were ours.
- `publicDomain: true` means the cited page supports a folklore layer, a historical person, or an explicit public-domain statement (the 1897 *Dracula* text in the United States; the 1968 *Night of the Living Dead* film because the copyright notice was missing). It does not clear later film costumes.
- `confidence` is `high` on 82 records and `medium` on Hasshaku-sama (`INS-05-064`), because that figure is a recent anonymous post and the copyright status is not settled.
- `sensitivity.rating` is `low`, `medium`, or `high`. High means do not build comedy on the cited harm. Several high records exist so later agents do not "discover" them without the warning.

Counts at this writing: **84** inspirations, **89** source rows, **88** distinct URLs. Kinds: anime 28, pop 19, urban legend 17, folklore 10, historical 10.

## What makes these archetypes work

The usable piece is almost always a **structure**, not a costume.

- A boss needs a **readable silhouette** before the name. Blackbeard's reported hat-fuses, Krahang's rice baskets, a headless rider, a hook in a raincoat, and a machine wearing skin all read at card size. The protected versions of those silhouettes (a specific pirate film, Karloff's flat head, the T-800 skull, Siren Head's megaphones) do not.
- A boss needs a **rule the player can learn**. Gremlins posts three care rules. Aka Manto offers two answers that both lose. Kuchisake-onna asks one question with no safe reply. Faust signs a price. Devil Fruits grant a power and take swimming away. The exact rules in those works are protected or culturally sharp; the idea of a posted rule is not.
- A boss needs a **reason the second phase is different**, not just larger numbers. See below.
- A final boss can be funny and still be larger than a normal enemy. The Compact Tyrant pitch is a receptionist who unpacks. The joke is the unpacking. The scale is the second body.

## Entrances

Patterns that showed up across the sources:

1. **The call.** Kamen Rider starts on "Henshin" and a belt. Hanako-san answers a knock on a numbered stall. Bloody Mary answers a repeated name in a mirror. Aka Manto speaks only after you are already in the room. A tower entrance can be a word, a knock, or a form that has to be filled in before the sprite appears.
2. **The leap or the dock that never happens.** Spring-heeled Jack's 1837 reports are about an arrival that ignores distance. The Flying Dutchman is an arrival that never completes. Both are entrances: one too sudden, one too late.
3. **The scale change as the entrance of phase two.** Super Sentai's typical episode defeats the monster, then fights it again after it enlarges, with mecha. That enlargement is an entrance, not a new character. Ultraman's Color Timer is the opposite entrance: the giant is already here, and the light says how long it may stay.
4. **The crowd that was already the boss.** Devilman's demons hide in human society. Tokyo Ghoul's predators look like people until the eyes change. The Thing assimilates and then imitates, so the entrance already happened. A vanishing hitchhiker is a passenger who was never entirely in the car.
5. **The small polite shape.** Frieza's earlier forms are described as suppressions of a simpler true body. Mazinger's pilot climbs into a machine that was built in secret. The Terminator is tissue over an endoskeleton. The entrance lie is "this is the whole creature."
6. **The sound before the body.** Teke Teke's name is the scraping. Siren Head, which must not be copied, is a lure made of borrowed voices. A parody entrance can be a noise that arrives one floor early, without copying either design.
7. **Theatrical smoke.** Blackbeard was reported to tie lit fuses under his hat. The fact is "reported," not proven. The pattern is an entrance that is mostly the costume being on fire in a controlled way. Aegis Dominion, the tank faction, would be the ones trying to put it out.

## Defeat tropes

- **The finisher waits.** Kamen Rider's Rider Kick is described as a flying kick used once the kaijin is already weakened. The trope is a named closer that is illegal until the opening has happened. Invent the closer.
- **The weapon is not what ends it.** The King Kong article quotes the 1933 line that it was not the airplanes. The trope is a defeat caption that points at a relationship, not at the last hit. Do not reuse the line.
- **Refuse the menu.** Aka Manto kills you for either color. Survival is rejecting both. That is a defeat of the boss's rule, and it is a better comedy button than a gory punishment.
- **The posted weakness is environmental.** Sunlight kills the mogwai in *Gremlins*. Sunlight, a special alloy, or wisteria ends demons in *Demon Slayer*. Sunlight-versus-monster is older than either work. Copying either work's full kit is not.
- **Exhaust the hidden battery.** Fullmetal Alchemist's homunculi fall when the philosopher's stone is used up. Ultraman's minutes run out. A tower boss can lose because the thing powering the phase is spent.
- **Bar the door.** *Night of the Living Dead* is a siege by a horde. The 1968 film is the one the article says is public domain. Later zombie films are not. The defeat is carpentry and dawn, not a trademarked virus.
- **The fleet was the boss.** Zheng Yi Sao's article gives a scale of about 400 junks and tens of thousands of people. The defeat of that kind of boss is breaking the roll call, not dueling a hat.
- **The map was the monster.** Captain Kidd's buried treasure is a belief that outlived the man and caused centuries of hunts. A defeat can be finding the hole and nothing else.
- **Do not "defeat" a real atrocity.** Vlad III, Mary I's burnings, Elizabeth Báthory's accusations, the Pied Piper's children, La Llorona's children, the Pope Lick trestle deaths, the Mothman-linked bridge disaster, and Godzilla's nuclear context are not win animations.

## Urban-legend and creepypasta provenance

Use a figure only when the record can say where it came from.

Clear provenance in this set: Slender Man (Eric Knudsen / Victor Surge, Something Awful, 10 June 2009, and explicitly **not** public domain); Candle Cove (Kris Straub, 2009, Creative Commons, which is still not a commercial-game license); The Backrooms (anonymous 4chan caption, 2019, on a 2002 photo; later series are copyrighted); Siren Head (Trevor Henderson, 2018, protected design); Ben Drowned was fetched and then left out of the inspiration list because it is welded to a Nintendo game; Hasshaku-sama (anonymous post, 26 August 2008, copyright unclear, confidence medium); SCP Foundation (collaborative wiki that accidentally adopted CC BY-SA 3.0 on Wikidot in 2008).

Older urban legends with a dated print or rumor trail: Spring-heeled Jack (1837), the Hook (Dear Abby, 1960), Aka Manto (schoolyard rumor by the 1930s, via Matthew Meyer), Bloody Mary mirror rite (modern form coalescing in the late 1960s; the link to Mary I is only a candidate), chupacabra (Puerto Rico, 1995; the spined look is tied by researchers in the article to the film creature Sil, which is copyrighted).

## Historical legends versus real harm

- Vlad III is a real 15th-century ruler. The article says it is only **suggested** that his patronymic fed Stoker's name, and that Vlad is not directly in Stoker's notes. Count Dracula the novel character is a separate record, public domain in the United States, and still not a license for film capes.
- Mary I's nickname and the mirror ghost are easy to mash together. They are `INS-05-073` and `INS-05-027`. Do not mash them.
- Elizabeth Báthory's blood-bath is, on the cited page, a rumour written down more than a century later and considered unreliable by modern historians. She is not a boss.
- Pirate legends that are safe to echo at archetype level: Blackbeard's reported burning-fuse silhouette (not the slave ship), the Flying Dutchman that cannot dock, Davy Jones's locker as a sailors' euphemism from 1726 (not the film captain), Zheng Yi Sao's fleet scale, Anne Bonny via Johnson's 1724 book with the uncertainty kept visible, and Kidd's treasure maps. Film pirates are someone else's costumes.
- The Pied Piper's rats and unpaid bill are the comedy door. The children are not.

## World fit, without changing lore

The locked frame this research sits on:

- Comedy fantasy. No compulsory main story.
- Four factions, names unchanged: Aegis Dominion (tank), Phantom Blades (DPS), Celestial Nexus (magic), Sanctuary Order (support).
- The Mysterious Tower is the PvE place. A monster may be a Wild Creature, a Corrupted Echo, a Tower Guardian, or a tower anomaly. It does not have to join a faction.
- Hybrid avatars are real people plus an Echo. A boss can be an Echo that kept the job and lost the person, or a wild thing the Tower grew. That is a use of the existing idea, not a new rule.
- Starter tone is already punning and domestic (a pot-lid tank, a squeaky blade, a meteor, a plaster support). Bosses can be that domestic and still be the largest thing on the floor.

No pitch below adds a stat, a drop rate, or a lore revision.
