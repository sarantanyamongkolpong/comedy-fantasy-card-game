## How to read a record

- `facts` and `sources` are research. If a detail is missing, it was not in the page that was fetched. It must not be backfilled from memory.
- `parodyPotential` is an agent comment about whether a comedy card is safe.
- `sensitivity`: `low` (folkloric monster, no living cult in the sources read), `medium` (auspicious symbol, shrine-adjacent custom, or a being some sources call a spirit/deity but which is mainly a folk character), `high` (deity, living worship, national or imperial sacred symbol, or a child-spirit practice). High means do not build a joke that mocks the worship.
- `confidence`: `high` when a fetched encyclopedia page states the point plainly; `medium` when the page is short, the tradition is disputed in that same page, or only part of the usual story was actually on the page.

## Batch 1 cautions

- Jiangshi hopping is the cinema image. Folklore sources describe running, and sometimes flight. Corpse-driving is the historical practice behind the hop.
- Nian: written creature-sources start in the early 20th century. Treat the ancient-myth claim as uncertain.
- Do not parody Sarutahiko (named as the model of the long-nosed tengu), Takemikazuchi (the god who pins Namazu), Raijin, Ibuki Daimyōjin / the Kusanagi myth around Yamata no Orochi, or the enshrined onryō Masakado, Michizane, and Sutoku.
- Bai Suzhen is sometimes worshipped as Madam White Snake. Affection only.
- Mogwai is a Cantonese word for demon. It is not the 1984 film creature.
- Kitsune powers beyond shapeshifting or bewitching were not in the lead that was read. Inari was not sourced here.
- Kasa-obake: the one-eyed, one-legged look was not in the lead. Gashadokuro is filed by the source as modern, and only the rattling skeleton that picks people up was confirmed.
- Yuki-onna: regional names only. No ice-breath fact was fetched.
