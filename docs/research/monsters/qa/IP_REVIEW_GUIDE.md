Status of this file: **RESEARCH**. It is a checklist for agents 01–08 and for QA. It does not clear a name for the live game, and it does not change locked lore.

The Owner brief says parody names should be recognisable and should have their own identity. It also says never to copy artwork, protected proper names, or original character details verbatim. The bands below are how this project applies that brief. A tool hit is a reason to rename or redraw. It is not a finding that a law was broken.

This game is a commercial card game. A monster’s name is how the card is identified in the product. That is closer to the Bad Spaniels problem than to a song that comments on another song. A public-domain myth can still sit underneath a living studio’s costume, title, or character design. Those are different layers. The checklist separates them.

Agent 10 is not a lawyer, and this file is not advice to the Owner about Thai or U.S. law. WIPO’s FAQ says copyright is territorial and points people to national law. A register search and a lawyer are outside this research pass.

## Three checks, kept separate

1. **Expression.** A specific design, illustration, costume, silhouette, or scene. The Copyright Office FAQ protects the way an idea is expressed. The idea of “a slime” or “a knight” is not that expression.
2. **Brand.** A word, title, or symbol players use to recognise a company or a character as the source of goods. USPTO’s confusion factors are the ones this tool approximates: spelling, sound, and shared distinctive tokens. The tool does not query the trademark register.
3. **Editorial sensitivity.** Living objects of worship, real mass deaths, and ethnic caricature. This is a project rule. It is not an IP-band score. WIPO’s traditional-knowledge page is the cited background for treating cultural expressions as something communities care about, not as free comedy props.

## Low

Use low when a player can feel the inspiration and the card still stands as its own character.

- The name is coined. It does not contain a franchise title or a character’s name.
- Edit similarity against the inspiration’s distinctive name stays under 0.72, and the shared prefix is short.
- The visual uses the creature type (snakes for a gorgon, a maze for a bull-headed guard) and invents the job, costume, and silhouette.
- The art prompt does not say “in the style of” a living studio and does not list that studio’s signature cues.
- A public-domain story can be the research fact. The card name is the new identity.

Examples that sit in low for this project:

- Inspiration “slime”, which is a generic creature word. Card name **Custard Knight**. Body: a cube of custard with a spoon for a shield. The word slime is not a protected proper name. A teardrop, blue, smiling blob is a different question and is called out under high.
- Inspiration: the public-domain minotaur story. Card name **Receiptaur**, a bull-headed clerk in a labyrinth of unpaid invoices. The bull and the maze are the myth. The job and the papers are the new character.

Low is still a research record. The `research` block keeps the sourced facts and the URL. The `creative` block keeps the coined name. Do not paste the franchise title into the lore summary to “help the artist.”

## Medium

Use medium when the joke is recognisable because the old name is still doing the work.

- Compact-name edit similarity against the inspiration is at least 0.72 and below 0.88.
- The parody keeps a shared prefix of 6 or more letters that covers at least 68% of the longer string. **Sephiroast** against Sephiroth is the example the tool is built to catch: the player is still reading Sephiroth.
- A public-domain name is used as the product name. **Medusa** on the card is the myth’s name, so the character has not grown its own identity yet, even though the story is old.
- The phonetic skeleton matches and the spelling is also close.
- One distinctive token of a longer name is shared (`cloud` is too common to count; a token of 5 or more letters that is not in the generic list counts).
- An ambiguous English word that is also a character (`Link`, `Peach`, `Toad`) is the entire name. That is medium, because the word is ordinary English and also a character. **Dark Link** is not medium. A modifier glued onto the character is high.
- A signature visual matches all but one of its cue groups.
- The record’s own `ipSimilarityRisk` says low and the heuristic says high, or the reverse. The human number and the tool number need another look.

What to do: move the wordplay one step so the card still makes sense to a player who has never met the original. Keep the myth or the archetype. Drop the stem of the modern name.

## High

Use high when the card is still the other work’s name or the other work’s picture.

- The parody name is the same string as a character, house title, or `protectedName: true` inspiration. That is also **P0** in the QA report. **Pikachu** as the card name is P0.
- Edit similarity against a review-list name is at least 0.80, or at least 0.88 against the stated inspiration.
- The longer name only adds two characters or fewer after a long shared prefix, and that prefix is at least 75% of the longer string. **Pika-chew** is high on that rule. It is P1, not P0, because it is not the identical string.
- A house mark or a non-ambiguous character name appears as a whole token in the name, Thai name, boss title, ability, mechanic, joke, lore, or prompt. The list includes titles such as Pokémon, Nintendo, Final Fantasy, Dragon Quest, The Legend of Zelda, Disney, Marvel, Genshin, and character names such as Sephiroth, Mario, Goku, Naruto, Luffy. The list is a review heuristic of names widely used as brands. It is not a statement that a particular registration exists.
- Thai spellings of those names are on the same list (`พิคาชู`, `โปเกมอน`, `มาริโอ`, `เซฟิรอธ`, `นารูโตะ`, `โกคู`, `ลูฟี่`).
- The art prompt matches a full signature set. The electric-mouse set is: yellow, a mouse or rodent, red cheek circles, and a lightning-bolt tail. Three of those four is medium. All four is high, even if the name is **Sparky**.
- The prompt says “in the style of”, “official art of”, “same costume as”, or “on-model with” a studio or a franchise.
- A generic word repeated with a coat of paint is not high by itself. **Slime** named **Slime** is medium: no identity, and the word is generic. **Dark Link** is high: the modifier does not make a new character.

Other signature sets the tool looks for, all of them combinations rather than a single common word:

- blue hedgehog, red shoes, white gloves
- red cap, mustache, blue overalls
- blue teardrop slime with a simple smiling face
- spiky hair, orange gi, and a flying nimbus or the series’ attack name
- straw hat plus a rubber stretching body
- black full-face helmet, cape, and a breathing apparatus
- red-and-blue web mask
- white hair, two swords, wolf medallion
- bald head, red tattoos, chained blades
- green tunic and pointed cap plus a Triforce or Master Sword
- a transformation compact plus a ribbon costume plus a second pose (a school uniform, wand, or tiara on top of those three is high)

A single cue is not a hit. “Red cap” alone is not Mario. “Straw hat” alone is not a pirate from that series. The combination is the flag.

## What the tool measures

The numbers live in `tools/research/monsters-qa/lib/ip-check.mjs` as `RISK_THRESHOLDS`.

| Test | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|
| Edit similarity of the compact parody name against the inspiration | ≥ 0.72 | ≥ 0.88 |
| Same test against a non-ambiguous name on the review list | ≥ 0.72 | ≥ 0.80 |
| Shared prefix of at least 6 letters covering at least 68% of the longer string | medium | high when the leftover is 2 characters or fewer and the prefix is at least 75% |
| Phonetic key (Latin, vowels kept, close consonants folded) | match, and edit similarity also ≥ 0.72 pushes a list-name to high | |
| House mark or character token in a player-facing field | | high |
| Ambiguous English token that is the whole name (`Link`) | medium | high if the only extras are modifiers (`Dark Link`) |
| Signature visual groups | partial group count | full group count |
| “In the style of” plus a studio or franchise | | high |

Compact form lowercases, strips accents and apostrophes, and deletes spaces and punctuation. `Pika-chew` and `pikachew` are the same string for the edit test.

Generic tokens do not count as distinctive: slime, goblin, dragon, knight, shadow, fire, echo, and the other words in `GENERIC_TOKENS`. Two slimes are not an IP hit because both are slimes. They can still be a duplicate, which is the dedupe tool.

The tool does not read `origin` or the research `facts` as a violation. Those fields are where the inspiration is supposed to be named. It does read the name, the Thai name, the boss title, the ability, the joke, the lore summary, and the art prompt. A lore sentence that says the monster “is basically Sephiroth” is a high hit. Move that sentence into `research`.

`protectedName: true` on the inspiration, or a review-list identity, makes an exact copy **P0**. `publicDomain: true` with the same name and no review-list hit stays **medium**: the project still wants its own name, and the tool is not calling the myth a trademark. `protectedName: false` on an exact non-generic name is high and P1, not P0.

Locked names from v0.5 are a separate collision, priority P1: Aegis Dominion, Phantom Blades, Celestial Nexus, Sanctuary Order, Mysterious Tower, Justin Case, Justin Time, Sir Potlid, Nox Squeak. They are not parody fodder.

## Sensitivity (not an IP band)

The sensitivity list is editorial. A high flag has to be revised before a record is marked QA_PASSED. It is recorded as P1, not as an IP-risk high, so a clean public-domain name is not confused with a trademark problem.

**High on a creative name, title, joke, or ability**

- Names of figures worshipped in living religions, used as the monster: Allah, Yahweh, Muhammad, Jesus Christ, the Buddha, Ganesha, Shiva, Vishnu, Krishna, Durga, Lakshmi, Guanyin. Thai spellings of those names are on the list.
- Kali or Amaterasu as the creature’s name. The lexicon starts them at “review” because the words also occur in other contexts; using one of them as the card name is raised to high.
- Skinwalker, including the Navajo phrase. Do not research this into a monster.
- A real mass death as the joke or the name: Holocaust, Auschwitz, 9/11, Sandy Hook, Columbine, Khmer Rouge, the Nanjing Massacre.
- Ethnic caricature: slanted eyes as the joke, blackface, yellowface, “ching chong”, “gypsy” as the creature type, “savage tribe”, and a nationality or religion paired with greedy, lazy, dirty, or terrorist.

**Note, not a failure, on a research record**

Agents 01–05 are supposed to name the myth they studied. A fact that says Ganesha is depicted with an elephant head is a note so the designer does not promote that name onto the card. It is not a P0 against the researcher.

Zeus, Thor, Anubis, and the rest of the widely retold pantheons are not on the automatic list. Copying a film costume onto Thor is still an IP-visual problem. The myth name itself is a medium identity problem if it is also the card name, under the public-domain rule above.

Hiroshima, Nagasaki, and “twin towers” are review, not an automatic high, because the words also appear in ordinary historical sentences. If the comedy concept is the joke, treat it as high and replace it with a fictional tower accident.

## How a record should come back

| Tool result | What the author does |
|---|---|
| P0 source | The URL is dead, unrelated to the claim, a placeholder, or a search page. Replace it with a page that actually states the fact. Do not mark the fact sourced. |
| P0 name | The card name is the protected or brand-distinctive name. Coin a new one. Leave the original in `research` only. |
| P1 | High similarity, a blocked or partial source, a sensitivity high, a canon-name collision, or a file that is not readable. Revise before QA_PASSED. |
| P2 | Medium name distance, a shared inspiration another agent also filed, or a sensitivity “review”. Merge or push the joke further. |
| Low, no flag | Eligible to stay in the sample. QA_PASSED is still a later human mark. Never write LOCKED or OWNER_APPROVED. |

Suggested fixes in `ip_audit.json` are directions. They are not replacement names. The agent who owns the file writes the new name.

## Source checker, beside the name checker

`check-sources` fetches each URL and records status, final URL, title, and whether the page text contains the distinctive words of `supports` (or of the facts, when `supports` is thin). A research inspiration also has to show its own name on the page.

| Verdict | Meaning | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| verified | 2xx, not a bot wall, and the key terms are on the page | none |
| partial | the page loaded and only some of the claim is there, or a title/host mismatch capped the result | P1 |
| unrelated | the page is about something else | P0 |
| dead | 404, 410, a soft 404 title, or the host does not resolve | P0 |
| suspicious | placeholder host, private address, or a search-results URL cited as the source | P0 |
| blocked | 401, 403, 429, a paywall, or a bot wall | P1 |
| unverified | PDF, a JavaScript shell, or a claim with no distinctive words | P1 |
| error | timeout or a 5xx | P1 |

A 403 is not proof the agent invented the URL. It is proof this pass could not confirm the sentence. An ENOTFOUND or a 404 on a cited source is unsupported. Unsupported sources are P0, as the brief requires.

Dedupe is separate again. The same parody name on two cards is P0. Two agents filing the same myth is P2: merge the research, do not design two monsters from one unmerged note. Concept overlap uses distinctive tokens only, so “fire slime” and “ice slime” do not collide on the word slime.

## Running it

From the repository root, on branch `research/m10-qa`:

```bash
node --test tools/research/monsters-qa/self-check.mjs
node tools/research/monsters-qa/cli.mjs check-sources --only-input --input docs/research/monsters/qa/ip_guide_sources.json --out docs/research/monsters/qa/ip_guide_source_check.json
node tools/research/monsters-qa/cli.mjs run --fetch
```

`run --fetch` fetches only `research/m01-classic-rpg`, `m02-western`, `m03-eastern`, `m04-world-myth`, `m05-anime-pop`, `m06-parody`, `m07-boss`, and `m08-lore`. It reads them with `git show`. It does not checkout those branches, and it does not write outside `docs/research/monsters/qa/` and `tools/research/monsters-qa/`.
